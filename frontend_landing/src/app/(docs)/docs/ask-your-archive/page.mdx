# Ask a question

<p className="lead">Ask about your bill in everyday words, and CloudLedger looks only at the accounts you can see, then answers with citations back to the underlying data.</p>

## Asking

Open the question box on the dashboard and type something like *“Why did EC2 cost more in
March?”* or *“Which service grew the most this month?”* CloudLedger searches the cost and
usage data it is allowed to read, then writes a short answer with numbered citations.

Each citation points to a specific finding, resource, or time window, so you can check the
source. An answer is built only from data that exists.

## When the data cannot answer

If the data does not cover the question, CloudLedger says so. It does not guess at a cause,
invent a saving, or describe a resource it cannot see.

> No data supports an answer to this question.

That is a safeguard, not a failure. Narrow the question, or connect the account that holds
the cost you are asking about.

## Good questions to ask

- “Which AWS service cost the most last month?”
- “What changed after the deploy on Tuesday?”
- “Where are we paying for idle resources?”
- “Which resources have no owner tag?”
- “How much did data transfer cost?”

## What the answer will not do

The answer will not change a resource, approve a fix, or spend money. It explains what the
data shows. Your team decides what happens next.
